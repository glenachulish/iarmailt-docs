# Iarmailt: Support

Iarmailt is a weather and hills app for hillwalkers and climbers: forecasts for
any point, a library on reading the sky, links to routes and conditions, and a
diary of the hills you climb. Free on iPhone, iPad and Mac.

## Getting help

Email **88_intake.troupes@icloud.com**. Say which device you are on and what you
were doing, and include a screenshot if there is anything to see.

## Common questions

**Is it really free? Do I need to buy it again for my Mac or iPad?**
It is free to download, with no subscription and nothing to buy inside it. One
app covers iPhone, iPad and Mac: download it from the App Store on each device
while signed in with the same Apple Account.

**Will my diary be on all my devices?**
Yes. Hills you log, notes, photos, pins and favourite webcams sync between your
devices through your own iCloud account, not through any server of mine. There is
nothing to switch on: if you are signed in to iCloud on the device, it is already
happening. Give it a minute or two after logging something. See the
[privacy policy](privacy.md).

**Where do the forecasts come from?**
You choose on the forecast screen: **MET Norway**, free and adjusted for the
height of the point, or **Apple Weather**. The mountain forecasts from MWIS, the
Met Office and the Scottish Avalanche Information Service are the publishers' own
pages, shown as they publish them.

**Why is there no freezing level?**
Neither forecast source publishes one, and a guess is worse than nothing on a
hill. The forecast screen links to the Met Office and MWIS pages, which give the
real figure.

**Apple Weather isn't working.**
Switch to MET Norway with the picker at the top of the forecast screen. Apple
Weather needs a connection and a device signed in to an Apple Account.

**How do I use a hill with no signal?**
Open the hill and tap **Save for offline** while you have a signal. It keeps the
hill's page, a forecast snapshot and a map picture on your device. The forecast
is only a snapshot and says when it was saved, so check a current one when you
can. Tap the download-arrow button on the map to see everything you have saved.

**How do I delete saved hills?**
In the **Saved hills** list, swipe a hill to delete it (right-click on the Mac),
or tap **Select**, tick several and delete them together. **Delete all
downloads** clears the lot. Deleting a download never touches your diary or notes.

**Why do the Outdooractive and OS Maps links leave the app?**
They open in the Outdooractive or OS Maps app if you have it, otherwise your
browser, centred on the summit. Those sites work better there.

**How do I log a hill I have climbed?**
In the **Notebook** tab, tap the plus and choose **Log a hill**. Pick one hill, or
several from the same outing, which are saved as a trip. Every hill is still
counted separately in your totals.

**How do I get my diary out of the app?**
In the Notebook tab, the plus menu offers **Export as CSV** and **Make a book**,
a printable A4 PDF of your hill days that you can filter by year, area or type of
hill.

**A hill or a link is wrong or missing.**
Tell me which hill and what is wrong and I will fix it in the next version.
Hill heights and lists come from the Database of British and Irish Hills, and the
Munros, Corbetts and Donalds follow the Scottish Mountaineering Club's tables.

**A webcam doesn't show.**
Webcam pictures come from the owners' sites, which change or go offline. You can
add your own on the Webcams tab with a web address that starts with https.

**Is it safe to rely on it on the hill?**
No weather app is. Iarmailt is a learning and planning aid, not a safety system.
Always check the official forecasts and avalanche reports and carry a map and
compass. The safety note is under **About and safety** in the map's layers menu.

## What Iarmailt does not do

No accounts, no subscription, no advertising of its own, no analytics. The
forecast, map and web pages need a connection; the hill information and the
Library do not.
